## Joins (Sam: "shouldn't bends be wider than the hole path, in ratio to the bend angle?")

Measured on the S7 bends of golden, heritage and townelake (n = 26). For each bend, the edge contrast was read in the
outer-corner wedge (the only place where join shapes differ) for round, miter and bevel joins at W, each model with its
best vertex offset (+-4 px) and half-width (0.85-1.15 x W/2). At turns >= 45 deg, bevel wins 9 bends, round 2, miter 2.
Both miter wins are bends inside a basket sprite (golden 4, heritage 9). On clean corners miter loses clearly:
- golden 8 (67 deg): miter -21 lum vs round 24, bevel 28;
- heritage 12 (85 deg): miter 48.7, round 55.7, bevel 63.5;
- heritage 11 (121 deg): miter 24 vs round 62.

So the corridor is not mitered: its outer corner sits at or inside the round arc. Bevel beats round by at most +8 lum in
the wedge, which holds a few percent of a route's band px (about 0.3 lum on a route). That is far below the 13-15 lum gaps
behind the swaps, so joins are not the cause and no join option was added.

## routeMatchKBends (rule set; ports/s7route/kbends.mjs)

Fair cost: a route is as good as its weakest leg (per-leg edge contrast, lum).
1. The assignment scores each cell by its best route with K <= 1 under that cost, plus the on-axis prior. B1 sits on the
   tee axis at or past the badge-at-0.57 point, which removes Coleto 11's phantom.
2. Every assigned route runs through the recorded pipeline (refine, second bend, placement, badge prior).
3. The holes still unconfirmed (sensor < tau) are re-solved together with K-bend chains, seeded from B* and from the
   K = 1 route. The weakest leg is split while a split makes progress, the best weakest-leg route is kept, and it is
   polished. A chain replaces a route only if its weakest leg AND its sensor reading are higher.

Why K-bend routes are not in the full table: with free bends, routes ride other holes' corridors. Heritage 4 -> Basket cc
(hole 5's basket) reads [43.6, 35.0, 41.5] lum on a 2-bend route (sensor 0.89), and the one-to-one assignment swapped
heritage 4/5. Holes the sensor confirms are therefore never reopened.

Dev (bench, testimony; agree / bends hit-missed-extra), routeMatchTeeAxisBadgePrior vs routeMatchKBends:
- golden 18/18, 10/0/0 = 18/18, 10/0/0;
- heritage 18/18, 18/1/1 = 18/18, 18/1/1;
- townelake 18/18, 3/0/0 = 18/18, 3/0/0;
- alex 3/3 = 3/3.

Validation, against Sam's calls:
- Coleto 10 -> Basket cc (1 bend) and 11 -> Basket ca (2 bends, sensor 0.96): fixed.
- Coleto 18: unchanged (the chain's weakest leg does not beat the K <= 1 route).
- Beaver 4: unchanged, still Basket cn. Beaver 19: still Basket cp, now with an OVERFIT 4-bend route around cn's rings.
- SeaTac 19: 3 bends, sensor 0.50. Its second leg cuts across the corridor: partly wrong.

Known limits: on synthetic zig-zags of 4-5 bends the greedy split stops at 2-3 bends, and unread (masked) legs count as
neutral, which lets a bend wander under sprites. Cost: the K-bend pass dominates (Coleto 97,508 leg reads, 43.7 of 52 s).

## routeMatchKBendsSeq (rule set; ports/s7route/seq.mjs; Sam: "18x18 is craycray. Think sequencing, neighborhoods")

1. S6-straight holes first: each claims the first forward BasketPoint on its tee ray (within 0.5 R1). Measured true on
   44/44 such holes.
2. Every other hole scores only its neighbourhood: 5 unclaimed forward baskets, a rank count. They are ranked by distance
   rank from the tee plus distance rank from tee N+1; the true basket's joint rank was <= 4 on all 71 bent holes.
3. Cells are scored by the weakest-leg cost, K <= 1 or the K-bend chain. A chain is tried only where the sensor rejects
   the K <= 1 route. It wins only if its weakest leg AND its sensor reading are higher, the sensor gaining more than
   0.05 per added bend (the recorded bend-count gate, bends.mjs).
4. Sam's rule "no passing through baskets", read as C1 discs per his correction: no leg comes within 1 R1 of a foreign
   BasketPoint. A basket whose disc holds the tee cap is exempt. Rejected cells are recorded with the basket crossed and
   the clearance in R1.
5. Claims are state passed forward; candidates, ranks and rejections go into `how` and `seq`.

Dev, Seq vs the accepted rule set: the same basket on every hole of all 6 courses. Agree and bends are identical:
- golden 18/18, 10/0/0;

- townelake 18/18, 3/0/0;
- lenard 18/18, 0/0/0;
- alex 3/3 (unjudged alex 13 goes from 1 to 2 bends).

## routeMatchKBendsSeq made fast (2026-10-21)

Profile (Coleto, before): the K-bend chains held 97 % of the work. Of 66 neighbourhood cells, 57 ran a chain: 224k leg reads
at about 0.14 ms each, half in split grids and half in refines and polish. The K <= 1 cells took 0.4 s.

What changed:
1. Batched leg reads (`fn.q.match.kLegsMany`): one store call per grid, refine step or sweep. The output is identical; the
   time did not move, because the cost is the reads, not the store calls.
2. Exact stops in each chain. The first leg is never split, so a route whose first leg reads no more than the best so far
   cannot win; and passFrac <= 1 bounds the per-bend sensor gate. Coleto 38.5 -> 29.6 s, identical.
3. Lazy chains priced by the assignment duals. A cell's chain is searched only if the cell is assigned, or if its hole's cap
   (the best first leg read on the tee axis, which no route of the hole can exceed) beats the cell's dual price. The bound
   exists and is exact, but it is one value per hole, so it prunes little (Coleto: 49 of 57 chains still searched).
   Priced-out cells are recorded as such in `how` and `seq`, never as rejected.
4. Coarse-to-fine. While cells are ranked for the assignment, chains split on a 1.87 R1 grid and refine to 0.5 R1. The
   assigned cells' chains are then re-run at full resolution (0.94 R1 grid, refine to 0.045 R1); Coleto needs 5 re-runs.

Result: the same basket, bend count and route vertices on every hole of the 9 courses (6 dev, 3 validation), and the same
judged agreement and bends. Time (bench, routeMatch with scene and table, 3 courses in parallel):
- validation: Coleto 39.0 -> 12.5 s, Beaver 47.9 -> 20.6 s, SeaTac 45.6 -> 17.2 s;
- dev: golden 9.5 -> 4.5 s, heritage 17.3 -> 7.7 s, townelake 6.0 -> 2.9 s, alex 12.8 -> 5.4 s, northpark 4.9 -> 2.7 s,
  lenard 1.6 -> 1.7 s.

Leg memo by quantized endpoints was not added. The reads are already memoized exactly by their endpoints
(`ribbonContrastAt`); quantizing would change the values, and leg-aligned split grids share few legs across cells.

## routeMatchKBendsSeq3: neighbourhood of 3, widened on reject (Sam: "cut at least 80% of the 18x18 cells")

neighbourhood ranks. A hole whose assigned route then reads below tau on the sensor, or that gets no basket, re-solves with
ranks 3-4 from the unclaimed pool (the S1-S3 fast pass / S4 recovers pattern). The route-match emits record
`cellsEvaluated`, `cellsFullTable` and `holesWidened`.

## Seq3 x region (lane s7region's sensor): measured, not proposed

The question: Beaver widens 11 of its 21 holes because the edge sensor gate rejects first-pass routes on a shaded course.
Does a region-read gate, or a region assignment score inside the neighbourhood, cut that widening and the time? And does it
unlock the 2-bend routes of Coleto 18 and SeaTac 19, which the edge gate rejects? Four rule-set options were measured
(match.mjs `seqGate` / `seqScore`). In all of them, bends are found and placed on edges, and the per-hole steps read edges.

Each variant run alone per course; results against Seq3 (edge gates):
- `seqGate: 'region'` (every gate of the sequencing reads the region passFrac):
  - widening cut: Beaver 11 -> 6 holes (73 -> 63 cells), heritage 4 -> 1, Coleto 4 -> 2, SeaTac 3 -> 1;
  - FAILS: golden 5 goes from 1 to 2 bends (agree 17/18, bends 10/0/1), and Coleto 15 / 18 trade baskets, leaving 18 at
    Basket cj with 5 bends.
- `seqGate: 'regionWiden'` (only the widening gate reads the region):
  - dev identical; Beaver's assignment unchanged with 11 -> 7 holes widened (73 -> 65 cells, 22.0 -> 21.8 s); heritage
    4 -> 1, SeaTac 3 -> 1;
  - FAILS on Coleto 18: its first-3 route to Basket cj (5 bends across open ground) reads hallway on the region, so the hole
    never widens to Basket ch (rank 4, Sam's basket).
- `seqScore: 'region'`: the same widening as Seq3, and the same Coleto 15 / 18 trade.
- both together: heritage 16/18 (14 / 15 trade baskets), and 4 Beaver holes change.

Coleto 18 and SeaTac 19: no variant unlocks their 2-bend routes. SeaTac 19 stays at 1 bend everywhere; Coleto 18 either keeps
Seq3's route (ch, 1 bend) or falls to cj with 5 bends.

Why: the region model reads light, smooth ground as hallway (regionSpace.mjs: 16-46 % of off-route cells read P >= 0.5). As
a GATE it accepts wandering routes the edges reject (render: s7bends/region.coletocreekFull.h18.jpg). The time is spent in
the chains, not the widening: Beaver changes by under 1 s.

## Why the edge gate "rejects" Coleto 18 and SeaTac 19's true routes (diagnosis, no code change)

How the routes were built: by hand along the visible hallway, then refined on the image. Each leg's centreline was fitted to
the measured ribbon centres (free edge pairs of width 0.8-1.25 W), then all bends were polished on the weakest leg.
- Coleto 18 -> Basket ch, 2 bends: S (717,1751), B1 (783,1446) at B* within 3 px, B2 (900,1258), ch (866,1171). Turns 18 / 46
  deg; legs 8.6 / 5.7 / 2.6 W.
- SeaTac 19 -> Basket ci, 3 bends: S (524,186), B1 (534,383) 16 px before B*, B2 (503,526), B3 (544,707), ci (538,832). Turns
  17 / 26 / 15 deg; legs 6.7 / 4.7 / 6.6 / 3.9 W. The visible hallway has 3 bends here, not 2.

The edge gate passes both true routes once their bends are on the ribbon:
- Coleto 18: sensor 0.86, weakest leg 24.9 lum. Its K <= 1 route reads 0.46 and 13.4 lum.
- SeaTac 19: sensor 0.94, weakest leg 43.9 lum. Its K <= 1 route reads 0.33 and 14.2 lum.

It is placement-sensitive. On SeaTac 19, moving bend 2 or 3 sideways by 0.25 W drops the reading to 0.54-0.62, and by 0.5 W to
0.35-0.53. On Coleto 18, fitted centrelines with bend 2 only 0.27 W off read 0.52. Moving B1 to B* costs SeaTac only 0.02
(0.90).

Per station, on the polished true routes (stations 1 px apart, the sensor's own verdicts):
- Coleto 18: 357 pass; badge masked 56; basket rings masked 19; ribbon wider than W 37 (median 1.28 W, from past the badge to
  B1); light ground beside 15; shade over the hallway 12; ribbon off the route 8; weak step 6.
- SeaTac 19: 379 pass; basket rings masked 68; badge masked 52; tee pad masked 7; light ground beside 13; ribbon wider than W
  8; off the route 2. Another hole's route beside: 0 stations on either route.

So the misses are in the SEARCH, not the gate:
- SeaTac 19: the greedy split from B* takes the B* -> ci leg's best split by mean contrast and lands on hole 20's parallel
  corridor: bend (419,810), turns 19 / 41 / 78 deg, weakest leg 19.3 lum, sensor 0.50. The zigzag is never reached, and the
  gated chain keeps 1 bend.
- Coleto 18: the chain compares candidates before polishing. Unpolished at B*, the first leg reads 8.2 lum, below the K <= 1
  route's 13.4, so the true route never becomes the best seen. Polishing moves B1 3 px and lifts it to 24.9 lum.

Bend geometry, a possible near-guarantee. Sam's dev testimony has 32 bends on 27 bent holes (golden, heritage, townelake;
0 / 1 / 2 bends on 45 / 22 / 5 holes):
- turning angles: min 14, median 35, max 72 deg;
- total turning per hole: at most 106 deg (heritage 9: 45 + 61);
- legs between two bends: 0.9-4.5 W (heritage 7 has a 0.9 W leg), so leg length is not a guarantee.
Against that:
- Coleto 18's region route to Basket cj: 5 bends, turns 22 / 86 / 96 / 42 / 112 deg (three above 72), total 358 deg; legs
  8.7 / 5.8 / 2.5 / 1.0 / 1.4 / 2.6 W.
- SeaTac 19's wrong chain: a 78 deg turn, total 138 deg.
On dev, true hallways turn by at most 72 deg per bend and 106 deg per hole. A per-bend sensor price of 0.05 lets a route spend
358 deg of turning when the gate is lenient.
